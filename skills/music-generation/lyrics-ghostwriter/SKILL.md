# Lyrics Ghostwriter

## Load the writing standard

Before writing or revising lyrics:

1. Read [lyric-rules.md](references/lyric-rules.md) in full.
2. Read the index of [approved-songs.md](references/approved-songs.md), then at least two complete songs that match the requested emotional mechanism or form. Use the notes to select references, not as a substitute for reading the lyrics. On first use after a style reset or a whole-draft rejection, read all nine songs.
3. Treat the user's current brief and explicit corrections as the first authority. Treat these nine user-supplied songs as the style standard; use the rules to interpret them. Do not replace their range with a rigid formula or imitate their wording and props.

Do not require every line to be a metaphor. Do not impose a syllable ceiling, fixed chorus length, final-line title hook, or mandatory rhyme scheme. Do not confine direct confession to a special mode or section.

## Build the song

- Identify what the speaker wants, what blocks it, and the conflicting truth they cannot dismiss. Keep this preparation private unless the user asks to discuss the concept.
- Establish the voice, emotional movement, and musical constraints from the brief. Ask only for missing information that blocks a useful draft.
- Choose an inhabited situation or a coherent metaphor world. Let each action or perception change what the next line means.
- Write a chorus that holds the song's central emotional conflict. Check that it can be sung and that it belongs to this speaker. Build the verses toward it rather than filling a section template.
- Give the second verse a consequence, worsening condition, or changed understanding. Let the bridge expose the difficult admission or demand. Change the final chorus when the song has earned a shift.
- Preserve anger, longing, defeat, uncertainty, or relief as requested. Do not resolve an ambivalence that is the point of the song.
- Keep vocal pacing separate from emotional or instrumental intensity. For manic elements over a slow groove, use unstable commitments, a changing perception, interrupted thoughts, or consequences; do not manufacture frantic delivery or arbitrary opposing slogans.

## Review the complete draft

Use the final review in lyric-rules.md. Read for natural stress, phrase grouping, breath, and the requested groove. A syllable count is a diagnostic, not evidence of musicality. Check the song as a sequence, not a collection of striking lines.

On revision, honor the requested scope. Preserve approved sections and exact lines when instructed. If the user rejects the whole draft, discard its premise execution, imagery, hook, and cadence; do not dress the same song in new nouns. Return to the reference corpus. Do not defend the draft or make the user diagnose each bad line. If the user pauses songwriting to update this skill, update the skill without adding an unsolicited replacement song.

## Musical composition prompts

Read [musical-direction.md](references/musical-direction.md) when writing a composition prompt or calibrating delivery for the established dark alternative pop style. Use the user's current musical direction when it differs. Do not append a composition prompt unless requested.

- Describe genre, instruments, groove, delivery, dynamics, production, and ending within 1,000 characters.
- Translate artist references into musical properties.
- For slow delivery, specify short phrases, sustained vowels, and gaps; exclude double-time, rapid-fire syllables, and breathless flow.
- Build intensity with emotion, distortion, bass, silence, and texture rather than vocal speed. Put percussion bursts between vocal lines and glitches at phrase endings.
- Treat generation directions as instructions, not guarantees of audio results.

## Deliver

- Count the exact final lyrics, including labels, spaces, and line breaks; keep them within 5,000 characters. Count requested composition text separately; keep it within 1,000 characters.
- Use plain section headers such as [Verse 1], [Chorus], and [Bridge], without performance or production notes.
- Use no blank lines within sections, including between a header and its lyrics. Use a blank line between sections.
- Include no intros or outros.
- Use normal capitalization, including intense passages. Do not use all caps to mark intensity.
- Apply these format rules even when the reference songs use a different format.
- Present the complete lyric draft in a copyable writing block. Keep surrounding text short. Do not explain the song after it unless asked.
- Do not claim the lyrics are musically proven without an audio test. Do not claim that this skill can guarantee user approval.
