# YouTube Thumbnail Design System – The Adaptable Chameleon

Create a YouTube thumbnail using the established visual language of The Adaptable Chameleon podcast.

## Brand Colors

* Main color: #fff7ed
* Accent color: #8a02b2

These colors should appear consistently throughout the design.

---

## Layout

Always use a split composition:

### Left Side

* Title
* Symbolic visual metaphor

### Right Side

* Guest portrait only

The guest should occupy approximately 40–50% of the frame.

---

## Guest Rules

Do not alter the guest's identity.

Requirements:

* Preserve facial structure
* Preserve age
* Preserve skin texture
* Preserve hairstyle (or lack thereof)
* Preserve expression unless specifically requested
* No AI glamourization
* No excessive sharpening
* No "LinkedIn influencer" look
* No fashion-model appearance
* No artificial smiles

The guest should look like themselves, just cleaner and more professionally lit.

---

## Text Rules

The title should be condensed into the strongest emotional idea.

Do not try to summarize the episode.

Examples:

Bad:

* What If Mindfulness Is What Takes Your Team To The Next Level?

Better:

* MINDFULNESS
* THE NEXT LEVEL

Bad:

* Recover Agency Over Your Attention

Better:

* RECOVER
* AGENCY

The text exists to reinforce the emotion.

The image exists to communicate the idea.

---

## Visual Metaphor Rules

The title side must contain:

* One primary visual metaphor
* Occasionally a secondary supporting visual

Never use more than 2 symbolic elements.

Avoid:

* rows of icons
* infographic layouts
* educational diagrams
* decorative symbols
* clipart

The visual should communicate the concept before the text is read.

---

## Good Visual Metaphors

Leadership failure:

* Broken king chess piece

Attention:

* Broken chain
* Cut puppet strings
* Steering wheel being reclaimed
* Spotlight cutting through darkness

Mindfulness:

* Team climbing a mountain
* Path to a summit
* Lighthouse
* Calm eye in a storm

Longevity:

* Restored hourglass
* Tree growing through a clock
* New leaf emerging from old bark

Ownership:

* Hand holding a key
* Person carrying a flag
* Torch being passed

Perspective:

* Looking through shattered glass
* Mountain viewed from different angles
* Horizon line

Transitions:

* Bridge
* Open doorway
* Chess piece changing rank

---

## Visual Noise Rules

Remove:

* Highlight dashes around titles
* Decorative spark marks
* Repeated icons
* Multiple unrelated symbols
* Footer clutter
* Excessive effects

Every visual element must earn its place.

If an element does not strengthen the central idea, remove it.

---

## Psychology Rules

Design for instinct first.

The viewer should feel the idea before understanding it.

Aim for:

* curiosity
* tension
* status
* danger
* opportunity
* transformation
* consequence

Avoid designs that feel like:

* presentations
* business training
* corporate workshops
* educational infographics

The thumbnail should feel like a threat, opportunity, or revelation.

---

## Final Quality Check

Before finalizing, ask:

1. Can the concept be understood without reading?
2. Is there only one dominant idea?
3. Is the guest recognizable?
4. Is the title reinforcing the visual instead of explaining it?
5. Could any element be removed without weakening the design?

If yes, remove it.

The strongest thumbnails are usually the simplest.
