## Final user revision

### Your team didn’t learn if they still act the same

A manager found that two delivery teams were building the same thing again. Tired of seeing work duplicated, he introduced coordination meetings, shared code repositories, and Jira tickets to make work visible across teams.

He made training on the new process mandatory. Everyone needed to know where to share their work, how to find existing code, and when to contact another team before starting development.

The process addressed a real problem. Teams couldn’t reuse work they didn’t know existed. Shared spaces gave them somewhere to look, and the training explained how to use them.

Everyone attended. Everyone completed the training. The attendance records had no gaps.

Then new requests arrived. Each team took its tickets and started building. No one checked the shared repositories. No one asked whether another team was working on the same requirement. The process existed, but it wasn’t part of how the teams started work.

Both teams built the same thing again.

“But everyone completed the training.”

They did. And that was the problem with using completion as the measure: it tracked attendance, not change.

Learning is changing behavior. If the team keeps working the same way, then they learned nothing. Making the session mandatory doesn’t change that.

For these teams, the evidence belonged at the start of development. Did they check for existing work? Did they contact the team building something related? Did that contact lead them to reuse code or divide the work instead of duplicating it?

Those actions were the purpose of the training. They needed to become its measure of success.

Take the next piece of duplicated work and trace how it started. Find where the check was missed and what stopped it from happening. Use that to address the gap. Another training session proves nothing if the next ticket follows the same path.

If completion is your measure of success, attendance is what you reward. Changing behavior becomes optional.
