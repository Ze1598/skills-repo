Turn the user's subject into one hand-drawn satirical editorial cartoon. The subject is the required input; choose supporting composition details without asking the user to repeat the style specification below. Ask for the subject if it is missing.

## Workflow: resolve geometry before style

1. Identify the central situation and visual irony. Preserve the user's subject. Do not add a second joke, a new narrative beat, or props that compete with it.
2. Check whether the camera position, character poses, gaze directions, and object surfaces can coexist. Determine which side of each important object faces each person and the viewer. Check what overlaps, what is hidden, and where hands or tools make contact.
3. Describe the proposed arrangement in three sentences: camera and framing; character positions, poses, and gaze; key object orientations and contact points. If readability conflicts with physical orientation, change the camera or composition. Do not twist objects to expose their contents.
4. Wait for approval of that arrangement before generating. Explain that this skill uses composition approval to catch angle conflicts. Reuse an arrangement approved in the current conversation. If the user asks to skip approval, perform the geometry check internally and proceed.
5. For scenes with several interacting people or objects, use a rough composition sketch before the finished image. Skip this extra image when the scene is simple or the user declines the sketch stage. For a complex scene, make the first generated image a sparse blocking sketch and obtain approval before rendering the final version; the sketch is an intermediate, not the cover.
6. Generate with the available image-generation tool. Put the approved spatial arrangement before the style specification in the prompt. For a final image based on an approved sketch, include that sketch as the composition reference. For a new image, do not use an earlier finished cartoon as an edit target unless requested.
7. Inspect the result against the approved arrangement and style. Check gaze, page faces, hand/tool contact, overlaps, object support, and readability. If the viewpoint is the cause of a failure, revise the composition rather than repeatedly patching hands. Offer the corrected arrangement for approval if it materially changes the approved composition; make a targeted correction without another approval for a local rendering error.
8. Deliver the image. Do not claim an exact pixel size without verification. Request 1080 × 1080; if the generator returns another size, use an available authorized resize/export route or state the size limitation. Follow the environment's image delivery and persistence instructions.

### Spatial example: attendance notepad

A manager cannot read or write on the back of a page while its written front faces the viewer. Use an over-the-shoulder or compatible three-quarter camera view so the viewer and manager can see the same written surface. The manager looks down at the pad; the pencil tip touches that surface and the support hand grips the edge or underside. Let the text appear foreshortened when the angle requires it. Do not retain an impossible front-facing display to maximize text readability.

Use this as a geometry example, not a required camera angle for every subject. Exaggerate anatomy for caricature while keeping spatial relationships coherent.

## Fixed visual specification

### Medium and character design

- Graphite pencil only on cream/off-white paper, approximately #F5F5DC, with visible natural paper grain. The cream substrate is the only tint; no colored marks.
- Traditional one-panel newspaper or magazine editorial cartoon. Characters must read as caricatures without context, not normal people drawn in pencil.
- Exaggerate head shapes, facial features, silhouettes, proportions, noses, chins, eyebrows, hairstyles, postures, and gestures. Heads may be oversized; torsos and limbs may be stretched, compressed, simplified, or angular.
- Construct faces with a few bold expressive features. Avoid naturalistic anatomy, conventional facial proportions, portrait detail, and cute mascot design.
- Use loose, confident HB–2B linework with visible strokes, natural line variation, some doubled or overlapping lines, intentional imperfections, and strong readable silhouettes.

### Cartoon language and mood

- Prioritize a clear visual idea over detailed illustration. Make role and personality legible through silhouette, face shape, posture, and gesture before fine details.
- Use visual irony, exaggerated gestures, simple exaggeration, and symbolic elements when they support the supplied subject.
- Make the central situation readable at thumbnail size; secondary details may reward a second look.
- Keep humor dry, observational, understated, and mildly absurd. Draw it from the situation, contradiction, or metaphor, not slapstick or a lecture.
- Critique the process or human dynamic in the subject without turning the manager or another character into a personal target.

### Shading

- Use at most three values: paper, mid-gray, dark graphite.
- Use simple hatching and occasional cross-hatching. Reserve dark accents for emphasis.
- Leave substantial untouched paper. Keep shadows loose and sketched; do not render every surface.
- No smooth gradients, airbrush effects, or digital shading.

### Composition

- Square, target 1080 × 1080 px. Main subject occupies about 60–70% of the frame.
- Centered or slightly off-center, generous negative space, clear hierarchy, minimal environment.
- Include only props and background elements that establish the situation or strengthen its joke. Do not fill available space for decoration.
- Keep paper texture visible throughout. No decorative border.
- Use only essential, brief hand-lettered text when the subject needs it. Do not add captions, slogans, or labels to explain a joke the picture conveys. No dominant clean digital typography.

### Execution and exclusions

Aim for a skilled cartoonist's quick editorial sketch, about 70% visual completion. Use fewer strokes, bold simplification, slightly unfinished edges, and visible construction marks when useful. Keep distortions intentional and the result authored and analog. Stop before it looks polished or overworked.

Exclude color marks, photorealism, realistic portraiture, naturalistic anatomy, digital painting, 3D rendering, corporate vector or stock-art aesthetics, perfect geometry or symmetry, polished line art, anime or manga, cute mascots, slapstick, meme layouts, excessive environment detail, heavy rendering, and unnecessary decoration. Do not include a signature.

## Final review

- Does the image preserve the supplied subject and its visual irony?
- Can the approved camera see every surface shown? Are gaze, grip, support, and contact physically consistent?
- Are figures unmistakable editorial caricatures rather than realistic pencil portraits?
- Are marks graphite, shading restrained, paper visible, and negative space generous?
- Is the situation readable at thumbnail size without extra explanatory text?

Keep this review internal. Geometry checks reduce errors; do not promise error-free generation.
