# Create Leadership Stories Thumbnail

Create one finished 16:9 YouTube thumbnail. Expect two inputs:

- one attached frame from the video;
- the exact video title in the message.

If either input is missing, ask only for the missing input. If both are present, create the thumbnail without asking for design confirmation.

## Series context

Leadership Stories is a series of short, story-led leadership videos for technically competent professionals. Each video uses a workplace case to expose a failure in communication, authority, ownership, incentives, decisions, or organizational behavior.

The thumbnail must feel business-facing but friendly to casual YouTube viewers. It must look like premium motion-design key art, not a corporate presentation, stock-business advertisement, or motivational poster.

## Fixed visual identity

Keep these elements consistent across every episode:

- exact 16:9 landscape format;
- near-black warm charcoal background;
- cream or off-white primary text;
- warm amber-orange emphasis and subtle glow;
- muted gray or cream diagram lines;
- crisp, flat, vector-like motion-design finish;
- generous negative space and safe margins;
- small uppercase amber series marker: `LEADERSHIP STORIES`;
- large bold condensed or geometric sans-serif title on the left;
- one simplified conceptual diagram on the right;
- no faces, people photography, stock imagery, logos, or watermark.

Use subtle texture or bloom only when it adds polish. Do not introduce blue corporate colors, strong gradients, excessive neon, or decorative clutter.

## Interpret the frame

Use the attached frame as the source of the episode's concept and visual vocabulary. Do not copy the full frame.

Identify the one conflict or mechanism that the frame communicates. Reduce it to one thumbnail-scale visual metaphor, such as:

- several people applying different labels to one object;
- one message reaching several people as different meanings;
- a decision moving through a broken ownership path;
- one person or process disconnected from the outcome;
- a simple before-and-after state.

Reuse recognizable shapes from the frame when useful: circles for people, diamonds for decisions or objects, thin connectors for relationships, and amber labels for conflicting interpretations. Remove captions, subtitles, role detail, and any element that will become unreadable at small size.

Do not turn the frame into an animated-slide screenshot. The thumbnail must communicate one idea in less than a second.

## Build the title

Preserve the exact wording of the user's title. Render it in uppercase and divide it into two to four compact lines.

Use this hierarchy:

1. Place `LEADERSHIP STORIES` above the title in small, widely spaced amber capitals.
2. Make the main title the dominant element and allocate about 55% of the width to it.
3. Select one key word or short phrase that carries the episode's tension and render it in amber.
4. Render the remaining title in cream.

Do not add a subtitle, hook, explanation, episode number, or other copy. Keep all text exact and legible at YouTube thumbnail size.

## Compose the image

Use the established layout by default:

- title block on the left;
- conceptual diagram on the right;
- balanced visual weight between both halves;
- no important element close to an edge;
- title remains readable when the thumbnail is reduced substantially.

The diagram supports the title. It must not compete with it. Limit diagram text to a few short labels taken from the frame's core idea. Make labels large enough to read. If the episode can be communicated without diagram text, prefer the cleaner result.

## Generate and validate

Use the available image-generation tool with the attached frame as a style and concept reference. Generate one polished image unless the user asks for variants.

Before returning it, verify:

- the aspect ratio is 16:9;
- `LEADERSHIP STORIES` is spelled correctly;
- the complete title is present and spelled exactly;
- the title dominates at small size;
- one meaningful word or phrase is amber;
- the right-hand diagram expresses the episode mechanism;
- no tiny explanatory text survived from the frame;
- the output matches the charcoal, cream, and amber series identity;
- there are no logos, faces, stock imagery, extra copy, or watermark.

If the generated text is wrong or the composition breaks the series format, correct it before delivery.
